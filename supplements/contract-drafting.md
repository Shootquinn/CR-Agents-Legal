# Contract drafting reference

Mandatory load for The Drafter and The Contract Editor. Load on demand for The Designer (sections 4 and 5), The Software Engineer (section 10), and anyone writing a compliance document (section 9).

This file governs operative text: provisions that create rights, obligations, conditions, or remedies, and the definitions they use. Prose surfaces (memos, issue lists, cover letters, explanatory notes) follow `writing-guides/style.md` and `writing-guides/structure.md` instead. The Manager assigns each surface at step open (operational guide A.3.3).

The organizing ideas come from two published bodies of work: Tina L. Stark's contract concepts (*Drafting Contracts: How and Why Lawyers Do What They Do*) and Kenneth A. Adams's categories of contract language (*A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting*). This file paraphrases and applies them. When a question goes past this file, the books govern.

---

## 1. Start from the deal

Before drafting, write down the deal in business terms: who gives what, who gets what, when, on what conditions, and what happens if it goes wrong. Each provision implements one piece of that list. A provision that implements nothing on the list is cut or raised as an open point. A piece of the deal with no provision is a gap.

A precedent form is a checklist of issues. Every provision inherited from a form traces to this deal or is cut.

## 2. Building blocks

Each piece of the deal is carried by one contract concept. The concept decides the remedy.

| Building block | What it does | If it fails | Typical form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement of fact (representation and warranty) | Allocates the risk that a fact is untrue as of a moment in time | Damages; possibly rescission; indemnity if the agreement provides it | "Seller states that, as of the Closing Date, ..." or the house construction for statements of fact |
| Covenant (obligation) | A promise to do or not do something | Damages for breach; specific performance where available; termination if material | "Supplier shall deliver ..." |
| Right | The other side of a covenant, stated from the holder's view | Same as the covenant it mirrors | "Buyer is entitled to ..." (use sparingly; usually state the covenant) |
| Condition | An event that must occur before an obligation arises or continues | The obligation does not arise; no breach unless someone also promised the event | "Buyer's obligation to pay is subject to Seller delivering ..." |
| Discretionary authority | A choice a party may make | None; it is a choice | "Buyer may terminate ..." |
| Declaration (policy, definition) | States how the agreement works | None directly; it governs how other provisions are read | "This agreement is governed by ..." |

Two tests. If the event does not happen, should the other side be able to sue? Then it is a covenant, or a covenant and a condition. If it should only excuse performance, it is a condition alone. And: does the remedy you would want match the block you wrote? A statement of fact gives damages for a false statement; a covenant gives damages only for a failure to perform.

## 3. Categories of contract language

Each sentence of operative text belongs to one category. The category fixes the verb structure.

| Category | Use | Verb structure |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | An act performed by signing the agreement | Present tense: "Licensor hereby grants ..." or "Licensor grants ..." |
| Obligation | A duty imposed on a party | "[Party] shall ..." where "has a duty to" fits |
| Obligation on a non-party or passive subject | A duty where the subject is not the party bound | "[Party] shall cause ..." or restructure so the party is the subject |
| Discretion | A choice | "[Party] may ..." |
| Prohibition | A duty not to act | "[Party] shall not ..." |
| Condition | A requirement for something to take effect | "To exercise the option, Buyer must ..." or "If ... , then ..." |
| Policy | Rules of the agreement itself | Present tense: "This agreement terminates ...", "A period ending on a non-Business Day ends on the next Business Day." |
| Declaration | A statement of fact, acknowledgment, or agreement | The house construction for statements of fact, chosen once |

"Shall" test: replace it with "has a duty to." If the sentence no longer works, "shall" is the wrong verb. "This agreement shall be governed by" fails the test; it is policy and takes the present tense.

## 4. Defined terms

1. Define a term once, in a definitions section or at first use, and point to the definition from the definitions section if defined in context.
2. Use the defined term in exactly its defined sense, every time. A synonym is a different term.
3. Capitalize defined terms consistently. A capitalized term with no definition is a defect. A definition that is never used is cut.
4. A definition states meaning only. A definition that contains an obligation, a condition, or a remedy is split, and the operative part moves to the body.
5. Do not define a term to mean something it does not ordinarily mean ("Products" meaning services).
6. Party names: one defined name per party, used everywhere, matching the preamble and the signature block.

## 5. Cross-references

1. Every cross-reference resolves to a section that exists and says what the reference needs.
2. Refer to sections by number; do not refer to "the foregoing," "above," or "below."
3. After any renumbering, every cross-reference is re-checked by script.
4. Exhibit and schedule references match the exhibit and schedule titles.

## 6. Ambiguity checklist

| Source | Check |
|---|---|
| Modifier scope | "Hardware and software products developed by Licensor": does "developed by Licensor" reach hardware? Restructure or tabulate. |
| "And" and "or" | Does "and" mean both together, or each? Does "or" mean one only, or one or more? Say which. Do not use "and/or." |
| "Including" | State once in the construction rules that "including" means "including without limitation," or decide each use. |
| Pronouns | A pronoun whose antecedent could be either party is replaced with the party name. |
| Time | "Within 30 days after" a stated event; "Business Day" defined; time zone stated where deadlines are hours; whether a period counts the first day. |
| Numbers | One form. No words-and-digits duplication unless the house style requires it. |
| Vague standards | "Reasonable," "promptly," "material," and efforts standards are deliberate choices. Where the deal needs a bright line, use a number. |

## 7. Usages to replace

| Usage | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "Shall" for policy or for a non-party | Present tense, or restructure |
| Doublets and triplets ("null and void," "indemnify, defend and hold harmless" by habit) | One word, unless each word adds meaning that has been decided |
| "Represents and warrants" by habit | One construction for statements of fact, chosen for its remedy |
| "Best efforts," "commercially reasonable efforts" undefined | A defined efforts standard, or a concrete obligation |
| "Provided that" | A separate sentence, or "but," "except," "if" |
| "Witnesseth," "now, therefore," "in witness whereof" | Plain lead-in and signature language |
| "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary" | A precise priority rule naming the provisions it overrides |
| "Herein," "hereof," "hereunder" | "In this agreement" |

## 8. Layout

1. One provision, one topic. Number every section and subsection.
2. Tabulate when a sentence lists conditions or alternatives; tabulation fixes modifier scope.
3. Headings are for navigation. State in the construction rules that headings do not affect interpretation.
4. The dash rule (operational guide A.12.6, Editor rule 12) applies to operative text.

## 9. Compliance documents (policies, training, forms)

Documents that impose rules on people inside a company follow sections 4 and 6, plus:

1. Every rule names who does what. A rule with no named actor is a defect.
2. Every rule names the instrument that carries it: a contract clause, a regulation, a company policy. A government-wide rule reaches a contractor only through its contract.
3. A rule with no outside source is a COMPANY-RULE. State it plainly; mark it in the tracking files; never dress it as a legal requirement.
4. A rule is stated no broader and no narrower than its source.
5. Every category of information (controlled, proprietary, export-controlled, and so on) is defined once, with its source, and no rule uses it in another sense. Where categories overlap, the document says how.
6. A signed form states no rule the policy or training does not.
7. Structure and necessity tests: Editor rule 19 (operational guide A.12.6).

## 10. Scriptable checks

These belong in every test suite for operative text, run by script (operational guide A.10, items 8 and 9), each with a seeded fault:

1. Every capitalized term is defined; every definition is used.
2. Every cross-reference resolves; every exhibit reference matches a title.
3. Party names match across preamble, body, notices, and signature blocks.
4. No "and/or"; no undefined efforts standard; no "shall" in a definition.
5. Dash grep: every em dash, en dash, and double hyphen is a number range or a hyphenated compound.
6. Echo sites (operational guide A.14) match their authoritative source.
7. Word and page caps, where the gameplan sets them.

## 11. Citations

1. Every cited case, statute, regulation, and clause is retrieved and on disk before the document cites it. The Fact-Checker treats an unretrieved case citation as fabricated.
2. Cite the version in force, with its date. Clause citations name the clause date (for example, "FAR 52.204-21 (Nov 2021)") where the contract incorporates a dated version.
3. A citation supports only what the cited text says. Interpretation is stated as interpretation and attributed to the persona who drew it.
