# Contract drafting reference
Mandatory load for The Drafter and The Contract Editor. The Government Contracts Lawyer loads sections 9 and 11 (operational guide A.3.2), and anyone writing a compliance document loads section 9. Sections 4 and 5 state the rules behind the defined-term and cross-reference checks The Designer receives as script output, and section 10 lists the scripted tests for operative text.

This file governs operative text (operational guide A.3.3, Drafting wave). Prose surfaces (memos, issue lists, cover letters, explanatory notes) follow `writing-guides/style.md` and `writing-guides/structure.md` instead, as The Manager assigns each surface at step open.

The organizing ideas come from two published bodies of work: Tina L. Stark's contract concepts (*Drafting Contracts: How and Why Lawyers Do What They Do*) and Kenneth A. Adams's categories of contract language (*A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting*). This file paraphrases and applies them. A rule neither book states is the team's practice. One statement-of-fact block merges Stark's representation and warranty. When a question goes past this file, the books govern; where this file and a book differ, the difference is raised as an open point.

## 1. Start from the deal

Before drafting, write down the deal in business terms: who gives what, who gets what, when, on what conditions, and what happens if it goes wrong. Each provision implements one of these: a piece of that list; an Author decision or COMPANY-RULE the gameplan records; or, on the counterparty's paper, a counterparty provision the issues list records as accepted. A provision with none of these is cut or raised as an open point. A piece of the deal with no provision is a gap. The list also states how the agreement works: what it supersedes, whether a party may rely on statements made outside it, how it is amended or waived, how notice is given, and which law and forum decide disputes. These are deal terms.

A precedent form is a checklist of issues. Every provision inherited from a form has a source under the rule above or is cut.

Each provision that allocates risk is marked in the drafting note and reviewed under the cross-review rule (operational guide A.3.3) before the step closes.

A term sheet, letter of intent, or deal memo that goes to the counterparty states which of its provisions bind (confidentiality, exclusivity, costs) and that the rest bind only in a signed definitive agreement. The drafting note says who may announce the deal and in what words. Statements, press releases, and conduct can still create an obligation the paper disclaims. The definitive agreement's integration clause names each earlier document it supersedes.

## 2. Building blocks

Each piece of the deal is carried by one or more contract concepts. The concept sets the default consequence of failure: a broken promise gives the other party damages, and a failed condition discharges the other party's duty. Stark's car sale carries one business goal with three concepts: a representation and warranty of the mileage at signing, a covenant limiting miles until closing, and a condition that the buyer need not close if the covenant is broken. The "If it fails" column states the defaults for statements of fact, covenants and the rights that mirror them, and conditions as the Restatement (Second) of Contracts states them, and for discretionary authority and declarations as Stark states them. The agreement's governing law decides each one, and the agreement's remedy, integration, and termination provisions can add to it or exclude it.

| Building block | What it does | If it fails | Typical form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement of fact (representation and warranty) | Allocates the risk that a fact is untrue as of a moment in time | Damages for breach of warranty. A misrepresentation claim, which can support rescission if the statement was fraudulent or material, induced assent, and the recipient was justified in relying on it; the governing law and the agreement's integration, non-reliance, and exclusive-remedy clauses decide whether that claim survives (COUNSEL). Indemnity if the agreement provides it; termination or a closing condition only as the agreement provides. | "Seller states that, as of the Effective Date and as of the Closing Date, ... Each statement in this section is a term of this agreement; if one is untrue, Seller shall pay Buyer's resulting losses under section [x], whether or not Buyer relied on it." |
| Covenant (obligation) | A promise to do or not do something | Damages for breach; specific performance if damages would be inadequate. After an uncured material failure, the other party may withhold its remaining performance; its remaining duties are discharged once the time for cure has passed, judged by the circumstances in Restatement (Second) § 242. State any termination right in the agreement, with its triggers, notice, and cure period. | "Supplier shall deliver ..." |
| Right | The other side of a covenant, stated from the holder's view | Breach of the covenant it mirrors, against the party who owes it | "Seller shall pay Buyer ..." (where the right is another party's duty, make that party the subject; "Buyer is entitled to ..." is the form to avoid; use it sparingly) |
| Condition | An event, not certain to occur, that must occur, unless its non-occurrence is excused, before performance of an obligation becomes due | The conditioned performance does not become due, unless non-occurrence is excused (for example, by waiver); no breach unless a party also had a duty that the event occur. State who may waive the condition and whether accepting late goods waives it. | "If Seller has not delivered the goods by 5:00 p.m. [time zone] on [date], Buyer is not required to pay for them, unless Buyer waives the delivery date in writing." An event that ends an existing obligation is what Restatement (Second) § 230 calls an event that terminates a duty, traditionally a condition subsequent; draft it with the termination provisions. |
| Discretionary authority | A choice a party may make | No claim for not exercising it. An exercise that does not meet its stated conditions (notice, timing, cause) has no effect, and a party that stops performing on it may be in breach of its covenants. State the conditions, the effect of exercise, and the standard (sole discretion, reasonable discretion, or stated criteria); state the notice form, any cure period, and whether termination limits accrued rights or other remedies; some governing law limits discretion by an implied duty of good faith and fair dealing. | "Buyer may terminate this agreement by notice to Supplier if ..." |
| Declaration (policy, definition) | States how the agreement works | None directly; it governs how other provisions are read. If a party would want a monetary remedy for the term, draft it as a statement of fact or a covenant. | "This agreement, and every claim arising out of or relating to it, whether in contract, tort, or under statute, is governed by the law of [state]." |

Two tests. First: if the event does not happen, should the other party have a claim? Then draft a covenant that the event happen, and add a condition if the other party should also not have to perform. If the event's failure should only mean the other party need not perform, draft a condition alone, in the condition form of section 3; a court reading doubtful language generally interprets it as a promise or a constructive condition, not an express condition. Second: does the remedy you would want match the block you wrote? A statement of fact gives a claim if it was untrue as of the moment it speaks; a covenant gives damages if it is broken.

For every set of statements of fact, decide whether the other party may rely on statements made outside the agreement, and if not, say so in a non-reliance provision. Whether that provision bars a fraud claim depends on the governing law (COUNSEL).

In a sale of goods, Article 2 of the Uniform Commercial Code as enacted in the governing state supplies remedies that displace this table where they differ: rejection, cure, revocation of acceptance, cover, the Code's damages measures, a buyer's duty, once it has accepted the goods, to notify the seller of breach within a reasonable time on pain of losing every remedy, and a four-year limitation period the agreement may shorten to one year (UCC 2-508 and 2-601 to 2-725 as Cornell's Legal Information Institute displays them). A seller's affirmation of fact or promise about the goods that becomes part of the basis of the bargain is an express warranty whatever verb introduces it, and the agreement limits that warranty only through the Code's rules on disclaimer, parol evidence, and limitation of remedy (UCC 2-202, 2-313, 2-316, 2-718, 2-719). Route every goods provision to The Commercial Lawyer.

## 2a. Sale of goods: Code defaults

In a sale of goods, Article 2 supplies default terms for many gaps the agreement leaves, and the principles of law and equity, including the law merchant, supplement the Code unless its particular provisions displace them (UCC 1-103(b)). Decide each default below, in the governing state's enactment, and record the decision in the drafting note. The Commercial Lawyer reviews each one.

| Default | Check |
|---|---|
| Battle of the forms (UCC 2-207) | Which document is the offer and which the acceptance; whether either limits acceptance to its own terms; which of the other side's terms come in or fall out; whether a master agreement states that it controls over later purchase orders and acknowledgments. |
| Express warranties (2-313) | Every statement about the goods, in the agreement, the specifications, or the sales material, is a candidate warranty. List the ones the deal intends. |
| Implied warranties (2-314, 2-315, 2-316) | Keep or disclaim merchantability and fitness. A disclaimer of merchantability mentions merchantability and, if written, is conspicuous; a disclaimer of fitness is written and conspicuous. "As is" language can exclude both unless the circumstances indicate otherwise (2-316(3)(a)). Course of dealing, course of performance, or usage of trade can also exclude or modify an implied warranty (2-316(3)(c)). |
| Exclusive remedy (2-719(1), (2)) | State whether a repair, replace, or refund remedy is exclusive, and what applies if it fails of its essential purpose. |
| Consequential damages (2-719(3)) | Exclude, cap, or keep. Decide whether the exclusion stands if the exclusive remedy fails. |
| Notice of breach (2-607(3)(a)) | Set the notice period and form, or accept the Code's reasonable time. |
| Limitation period (2-725) | Four years from breach. A warranty is breached at tender of delivery, except as 2-725(2) provides for one explicitly extending to future performance. The agreement may shorten the period to one year and may not extend it. |
| Good faith and reasonableness (1-302(b)) | These duties are not disclaimable. The agreement may set the standards that measure them, such as a number of days. |

Section numbers in the table follow the version of the Code that Cornell's Legal Information Institute aims to display, the version most widely adopted by states; check each against the governing state's enactment.

## 3. Categories of contract language

Each sentence of operative text is classed by category, and the category fixes the verb structure. The fifth edition of *A Manual of Style for Contract Drafting* has nine categories of contract language: agreement, performance, obligation, discretion, prohibition, policy, declaration, intention, and recommendation. It treats conditions apart from the categories. The table covers the categories operative text uses most. Language of agreement is used only in the lead-in ("The parties therefore agree as follows:"). A sentence that fits no row is raised with The Drafter.

| Category | Use | Verb structure |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | An act performed by signing the agreement | "Hereby" plus the simple present: "Licensor hereby grants ..." |
| Obligation | A duty imposed on a party | "[Party] shall ..." where "has a duty to" fits |
| Obligation imposed on someone other than the subject | A duty where the subject is not a party, or is not a legal person | restructure so a party is the subject first ("The parties shall hold the Closing at Acme's offices at [address]."); "must" ("The Closing must take place at Acme's offices.") names no obligor, so no party breaches if the Closing occurs elsewhere, and is used only where no party can be the subject; "[Party] shall cause ..." is a guarantee of the result, where a party is the subject and the object is a thing or the party's instrumentality |
| Discretion | A choice | "[Party] may ..." |
| Prohibition | A duty not to act | "[Party] shall not ..." |
| Condition (expressed through the categories) | A requirement for something to take effect | "To exercise the option, Buyer must deliver written notice to Seller by 5:00 p.m. [time zone] on [date]; if Buyer does not, the option lapses." or "If ..., then ..."; state what follows if it is not met. A duty that the event occur is a separate Obligation sentence. |
| Policy | Rules of the agreement itself, including definitions | Simple present: "This agreement terminates on ...", ""Business Day" means ...", "A date or period ending on a non-Business Day ends on the next Business Day." "Will" for a future event that might not occur or whose timing is uncertain: "This agreement will terminate upon the closing of a Qualified IPO." Define "Qualified IPO" and "closing", and state in the termination provisions what survives. The non-Business Day rule names any deadline it excludes (for example, the Outside Date). |
| Statement of fact (Adams's declaration) | A statement of fact, or an acknowledgment | "states" for a statement of fact ("Acme states that ..."); "acknowledges" for an acknowledgment ("Acme acknowledges that ..."). One verb for statements of fact throughout (section 7). An acknowledgment carries no duty to be true; use "states" where the other party needs a claim if the fact is untrue. |

Section 2's declaration (Stark's term) is this table's Policy row.

"Shall" test: replace it with "has a duty to." If the sentence no longer works, "shall" is the wrong verb. "This agreement shall be governed by" fails the test; it is policy and takes the present tense.

Tense does not set scope. "This agreement is governed by [state] law" may reach only claims under the agreement. A governing-law or forum provision states whether it reaches claims arising out of or relating to the agreement, including tort and statutory claims; that is a deal choice, reviewed by The Judge.

## 4. Defined terms

1. Define a term once, in a definitions section or at first use, and from the definitions section, point to each definition made at first use.
2. Use the defined term in exactly its defined sense, every time. A synonym is a different term. Use no word in more than one sense (Editor rule 9, A.12.6).
3. Capitalize defined terms consistently. A capitalized term with no definition is a defect. A definition that is never used is cut.
4. A definition states meaning only. A definition that contains an obligation, a condition, or a remedy is split, and the operative part moves to the body.
5. Do not define a term to mean something it does not ordinarily mean ("Products" meaning services).
6. Party names: one defined name per party, used everywhere, matching the preamble and the signature block.

## 5. Cross-references

1. Every cross-reference resolves to a section that exists and says what the reference needs.
2. Refer to sections by number; do not refer to "the foregoing," "above," or "below."
3. After any renumbering, every cross-reference is re-checked by script.
4. Exhibit and schedule references match the exhibit and schedule titles.

## 6. Ambiguity checklist

| Source | Check |
|---|---|
| Modifier scope | "Hardware and software products developed by Licensor": does "developed by Licensor" reach hardware? Restructure or tabulate. |
| "And" and "or" | Does "and" mean both together, or each? Does "or" mean one only, or one or more? Fix it where a party could argue the unintended reading without looking silly; leave it where nobody could. For "and/or," write "X or Y or both," or "one or more of A, B, and C." |
| "Including" | State once in the construction rules that "including" means "including without limitation," or decide each use; a court may read "including" as restrictive despite that rule. Where a list is meant to be complete, use "means" or "consists of." Choose examples that do not narrow the general word, because a tribunal may read it in their light. |
| Pronouns | A pronoun whose antecedent could be either party is replaced with the party name. A pronoun, "said," or "such" whose antecedent could be another noun (an amount, a period) is replaced by that noun. |
| Time | "Within 30 calendar days after" a stated event; "Business Day" defined; time zone stated where deadlines are hours; whether a period counts the first day. A point in time states the time of day. |
| Numbers | One form. No words-and-digits duplication except for a sensitive amount, such as a promissory note's principal. |
| Writing, signature, notice | Say whether email is a writing and a typed name a signature for amendment, waiver, and notice. Say when notice is effective (sent, delivered, or received), by what method, and to what address. |
| Vague standards | "Reasonable," "promptly," "material," and efforts standards are deliberate choices. Where the deal needs a bright line, use a number. An undefined "material" imports the governing law's case-law test, and whether the standard is met can be a question of fact. |

## 7. Usages to replace

| Usage | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "Shall" for policy or for a non-party | Present tense, or "will" for an uncertain future event (policy); "must," or restructure so a party is the subject (non-party) |
| Doublets and triplets ("null and void" by habit) | One word, unless each word adds meaning that has been decided. In "indemnify, defend and hold harmless," "defend" is a separate duty; keep or cut it as a deal term. Choose "void" or "voidable," and state whether "indemnify" reaches claims between the parties. |
| "Represents," "warrants," or "represents and warrants" introducing a statement of fact | "States"; any remedy for an untrue statement of fact (indemnity, termination, survival period) is stated expressly, with whether reliance is required. Where a counterparty's draft keeps "represents and warrants," add a sentence that the verb does not change the remedies the agreement states, and point to the section 2 form for the sentence stating the express remedy and reliance. For a sale of goods, see sections 2 and 2a. |
| "Best efforts," "commercially reasonable efforts," and other grades | "Reasonable efforts" as the one efforts standard, defined in the agreement; a flat obligation for any part of the task the obligated party controls (for example, submit a complete permit application no later than a stated date); and a list of actions the obligated party is not required to take, ending with any other payment or concession. |
| "Provided that" | A separate sentence, or "but," "except," "if"; a separate limiting sentence names the provision it limits ("Subject to section [x]" or "except that") and sits in the same subsection |
| "Witnesseth," "now, therefore," "in witness whereof" | Plain lead-in and signature language |
| "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary" | Decide whether the provision must override another and, if so, name the provision. |
| "Herein," "hereof," "hereunder" | "In this agreement" for the whole; the section number ("in this section 4.2") for a part |

## 8. Layout

1. One provision, one topic. Number every section and subsection.
2. Tabulate when a sentence lists conditions or alternatives; tabulation fixes modifier scope.
3. Headings are for navigation. State in the construction rules that headings do not affect interpretation, and put in the text every limit a heading suggests: a section headed "Exclusive Remedy" whose text states no exclusivity gives no exclusive remedy.
4. The dash rule (operational guide A.12.6, Editor rule 12, and A.12.10 rule 11) applies to operative text. The orchestrator runs the dash script and passes its hit list to The Contract Editor. He re-edits each listed provision whole for the writing failure the dash marks; a punctuation swap never clears a hit. The script then runs again on the changed provisions.
5. A provision the governing law requires to be conspicuous (a warranty disclaimer in a sale of goods, for example) keeps its conspicuous format through every layout and language pass. The Commercial Lawyer marks each such provision.

## 9. Compliance documents (policies, training, forms, procedures)

Documents that impose rules on people inside a company follow sections 4 and 6, plus:

1. Every rule names who does what. A rule with no named actor is a defect.
2. Every rule names the instrument that carries it: a contract clause, a statute or regulation that binds the company directly, or a company policy. A Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) or agency-supplement requirement reaches a contractor through its contract: a clause written into the contract in full text or by reference, or a mandatory clause that expresses a significant or deeply ingrained procurement policy, which is read in by operation of law when the contract leaves it out (the Christian doctrine; G.L. Christian & Assocs. v. United States, 312 F.2d 418, 424, 426 to 427 (Ct. Cl. 1963); K-Con, Inc. v. Sec'y of the Army, 908 F.3d 719 (Fed. Cir. 2018)). Whether a missing clause is read in is a question for COUNSEL. Some statutes bind the company whatever its contracts say; the civil False Claims Act reaches any person who knowingly presents a false or fraudulent claim for payment or approval (31 U.S.C. 3729(a)(1)).
3. A rule with no outside source is a COMPANY-RULE. State it plainly; mark it in the tracking files; never dress it as a legal requirement.
4. State a rule attributed to an outside source no broader and no narrower than that source. Where the company assigns a sourced duty to a role or extends it past the source, the assignment or extension is a COMPANY-RULE; mark it as one.
5. Define every category of information (controlled unclassified information, proprietary information, export-controlled information, and so on) once, by quoting or incorporating the definition in its source (the clause at the version the contract incorporates, or the regulation); no rule uses it in another sense. Where categories overlap, the document says how.
6. A signed form states no rule the policy or training does not.
7. Structure and necessity tests: Editor rule 19 (operational guide A.12.6), applied to operative text by The Contract Editor (A.12.10 rule 12).

## 10. Scriptable checks

These belong in every test suite for operative text, run by script (operational guide A.10, items 8 and 9), each with a seeded fault:

1. Every capitalized term is defined; every definition is used.
2. Every cross-reference resolves; every exhibit reference matches a title.
3. Party names match across preamble, body, notices, and signature blocks.
4. No "and/or"; no efforts standard other than "reasonable efforts"; no "shall" in a definition.
5. Dash script, run by the orchestrator: a hit is an em dash, en dash, or double hyphen used as clause punctuation. Numeric and date ranges, table rules and empty-cell placeholders, HTML comment delimiters, code spans and fences, and CLI flags are not hits. Each hit marks its provision for the full re-edit that section 8 item 4 describes.
6. Echo sites (operational guide A.14) match their authoritative source.
7. Word and page caps, where the gameplan sets them.

## 11. Citations

1. Every cited case, statute, regulation, and clause is retrieved and on disk before the document cites it. The Fact-Checker verifies each citation under operational guide A.12.8 rule 7.
2. Cite the clause version the contract incorporates, by number, title, and date, with "(DEVIATION)" after the date where the contract incorporates a deviation (FAR 52.101(f), 52.107(f)). Example: "FAR 52.204-21 (Nov 2021)," the version current at Federal Acquisition Circular (FAC) 2026-01. The dated version the contract incorporates governs. Unless otherwise specified, a FAR change applies to solicitations issued on or after its effective date, and a contracting officer may add it to an existing contract with appropriate consideration (FAR 1.108(d)). Clause numbers move, regulations are amended, and class deviations displace FAR text for the contract actions they cover (FAR 1.401, 1.404), until they end on their stated terms, for example incorporation into the FAR, supersession, or rescission. A rule proposed on June 23, 2026 states that the FAR Council is considering renumbering every provision and clause in FAR Part 52 (Federal Register (FR), 91 FR 37550, 37559). Where the version current on the retrieval date differs from the version the contract incorporates, state both.
3. A citation supports only what the cited authority holds or states. The document says which (holding, dictum, statutory text, or regulatory text) and whether the authority binds on the question in the jurisdiction whose law governs. Interpretation is stated as interpretation; the drafting notes attribute it to the persona who drew it.
